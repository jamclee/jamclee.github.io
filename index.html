Ph.D. Student in Marketing · Ross School of Business, University of Michigan

I study data quality and exclusions in behavioral research, how people perceive ratings and numerical information, and financial and health decision-making.

Hi there! I am a Ph.D. student in Marketing at the Stephen M. Ross School of Business at the University of Michigan.

Much of my research asks how researchers decide which responses count as data. Attention, comprehension, and manipulation checks are routinely used to exclude participants, but failing a check does not always justify exclusion. With Dan Schley, I am developing a framework that starts from the research question rather than the check, and a continuous, domain-general measure of survey engagement. I also study how consumers interpret star ratings (with Katherine Burson) and the psychological barriers to estate planning (with Carolyn Yoon and Scott Rick).

Before my Ph.D., I earned an M.S. in Information from the University of Michigan School of Information and a B.A. in Cognitive Science from the University of Pennsylvania. Along the way I worked in data engineering and user research at J.P. Morgan Chase, Levi Strauss & Co., Zappos.com, and Wharton Marketing and Communications.
